# Captions for the revised figure proposals

The filenames are stable identifiers, not assigned manuscript figure numbers. The two exposure graphics use real source inputs. The three result graphics and the supplementary event maps use the explicitly synthetic revised prototype.

**fig1_real_inventory — Real plant locations in the climate-extraction pilot.** Ten pre-specified WRI GPPD records selected by country/fuel stratum and largest recorded capacity, with source ID as tie-break. Names are direct labels; the figure does not imply representative portfolio sampling, current operation or insurance membership. WRI v1.3.0 is pinned to a January 2022 repository snapshot, CC BY 4.0. Country boundaries: Natural Earth v5.1.2, public domain. Source capacity and fuel fields remain available in the inventory CSV.

**fig2_historical_heat_spells — Coincident hot spells at sampled real plant locations.** Calendar strips show how many of the ten locations simultaneously belong to a hot spell in June–August of five pre-specified heatwave years. A spell is at least three consecutive UTC days with maximum 2 m air temperature above the site's seasonal 90th percentile, calculated from a centred 31-day window in 1991–2020. A 365-day reference calendar is used; leap day uses the 28 February threshold. The right-hand column reports the maximum simultaneous count within each displayed summer. Grid-cell sampling uses fixed ERA5, nearest-cell selection and disabled elevation downscaling through Open-Meteo. The count is equally weighted across ten sites, not an estimate of the fraction of European insured capacity exposed. Complete individual-site temperatures and thresholds are retained in the data files. ERA5: Hersbach et al. (2020), doi:10.1002/qj.3803. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information (2026), via Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0. Neither the European Commission nor ECMWF is responsible for any use made of the Copernicus information or data.

**results_equal_budget — Operating comparisons at numerically matched action spending.** Each line joins outcomes for the same synthetic book under a class-based operating rule and a condition-informed rule. Panels show mean annual covered loss and the 99.5th-percentile annual covered loss. All ten baseline books are displayed, ordered by class-rule mean loss. Highlighted reductions, 48.8% and 11.1% before rounding, are medians of paired relative changes, not changes in the ratio of medians. Spending is matched on the same simulated events, with maximum relative discrepancy 2.66e-6. Each book uses 20,000 synthetic years. The operating rules have not been tuned on training events and evaluated on a held-out sample.

**results_funding — Tail estimation and funding with owner operation held fixed.** Both underwriting models assess the same class-operation physical-loss target. Points represent scenario/book outcomes; diamonds represent medians and lines span minima to maxima. Panel a reports relative error in the estimated 99.5th-percentile annual covered loss. Panel b reports the percentage of synthetic years in which true covered loss exceeds modelled 99.5th-percentile funds, on a logarithmic horizontal scale. Dashed lines mark zero estimate error and the nominal 0.5% exceedance target. Baseline and warmer sensitivities use ten books; the optimistic-prior sensitivity uses six. The warmer case is the original synthetic +1.2 K/frequency sensitivity, not a CMIP6 projection. The optimistic prior shifts class headroom by +1.5 K; it is not observed stale insurance claims. Full condition evidence means the joint posterior with likelihood setting lam=1. The spread across these books is not a confidence interval or field validation.

**results_heat_severity — Operational protection across all simulated heatwaves.** For the seed-100 book, the step curve reports net failure reduction, (class failures minus condition-rule failures) divided by class failures, within each peak-anomaly bin. This includes newly occurring failures under the condition rule and differs from gross avoided failures. The count row reports event counts per bin on a logarithmic vertical scale; it uses points and stems because the anomaly intervals have unequal widths. All 30,062 events are included, including four at or above 20 K. The open-ended overflow bin is drawn only to the maximum observed anomaly. Pooled reductions are approximately 85% below 6 K and 8% at or above 12 K. Anomaly is measured at the synthetic event centre relative to the model reference, not above every asset's design limit. No smooth response function or confidence interval is fitted. This panel uses the original class and condition owner rules, not the equal-budget comparator.

**supplementary_event_maps — Loss-selected examples with no avoided failures.** For seed 100, events are selected whose baseline event covered losses are closest to the 10-year and 200-year annual-maximum occurrence-loss quantiles, independently of treatment benefit. These are loss-quantile labels, not meteorological heatwave return periods. Both panels have identical geographic extents and a shared synthetic anomaly scale. Grey points are synthetic assets; dark points fail in both operating arms. Covered losses are equal in the two arms in each selected example: EUR 8.84m and EUR 44.74m, with five and eighteen failures respectively. Both examples have zero avoided failures. Gaussian heat fields and random asset locations are illustrative. Basemap: Natural Earth, public domain.
