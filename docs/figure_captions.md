# Data diagnostic captions

**Figure 1. Recorded European power-plant locations and the climate-extraction pilot.** (a) Grey points show Solar, Nuclear, Coal and Gas records in the EU27, UK, Norway and Switzerland subset of WRI GPPD v1.3.0. Coloured points identify ten pre-specified sampling strata; each stratum selects the plant with largest recorded capacity, with source ID as a tie-break. Numbers link points to panel b and the rows of Figure 2. The map is cropped to show the pilot footprint; the archived inventory includes the full study-country subset. (b) Recorded capacity in MW on a logarithmic scale. This is a historical source inventory, pinned to a January 2022 repository snapshot. Current operation, plant-versus-unit boundaries, insured membership and insured values are unverified. “Solar” is the source fuel category, not a separately verified PV classification. Country boundaries: Natural Earth v5.1.2, public domain. Data: World Resources Institute, Global Power Plant Database, CC BY 4.0; the selection and visualisation are modifications.

**Figure 2. Reanalysis heat exposure and coincident hot spells at ten plant locations.** Top: daily maximum 2 m air temperature minus a site-specific seasonal 90th-percentile threshold for June-August in five pre-specified historical heatwave years (2003, 2018, 2019, 2022 and 2025). Thresholds are calculated from 1991-2020 ERA5 values within a centred 31-day calendar window, with a 365-day reference calendar and the 28 February threshold used on leap day. Bottom: percentage of the ten sites simultaneously belonging to a spell of at least three consecutive UTC days above their own threshold. Site weights are equal. The ten locations form a retrieval and visualisation pilot; these fractions are not Europe-wide exposed-capacity shares. The colour limits are shared across all years; values outside the scale are clipped and indicated by colour-bar extensions. Data are ERA5 reanalysis retrieved through Open-Meteo with a fixed ERA5 model, nearest-cell selection and elevation downscaling disabled. This is historical meteorological exposure, with no inference of observed failures, covered losses or the causal effect of adaptation. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information (2026). ERA5 citation: Hersbach et al., The ERA5 global reanalysis, doi:10.1002/qj.3803. Access service: Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0, https://open-meteo.com/.
